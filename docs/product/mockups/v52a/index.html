What the shape does and does not mean

Reading a curve

A curve is a price list, not a prediction.

Each point is what investors accepted on that day for lending to the U.S. government for that long. When the line tilts up, longer loans were paying more than shorter ones. When it flattens, that gap narrowed.

MacroChipz reports the shape and the dates it changed between. It does not tell you what the shape predicts. rates_v1.0 defines no state label, no forecast and no cross-domain conclusion — so this page does not say a flatter curve signals a recession, and does not say the 10-year yield sets the rate on your mortgage.

What MacroChipz calculated

Differences rates_v1.0 computes between published yields on one shared observation date. Inflation compensation can reflect inflation expectations as well as liquidity and risk premia, so it is not called an inflation forecast.

    The dashed curve is not reconstructed. Each maturity's response carries a from_date and a from_value for every session window, and those dates match across all four maturities — so the four published values for one date are plotted as they arrive.

    The widening-or-narrowing sentence reads the 2s30s spread's own published from_value and to_value. Nothing on this page subtracts one yield from another.

    Windows count published trading sessions, never calendar days. 21 sessions is approximately, not exactly, one month, which is why no window is labelled in months.
